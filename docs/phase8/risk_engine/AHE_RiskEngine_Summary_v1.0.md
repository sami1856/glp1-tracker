# Elisence – Advanced Health Engine (AHE)  
## Risk Engine & Population Intelligence – Summary v1.0

### 1. The Vision – From Reactive Treatment to Proactive Health

Elisence AHE is a next-generation **digital health intelligence engine** that transforms how countries, hospitals, and insurers see risk:

- From: *“We treat heart attacks, strokes, diabetes complications when they happen.”*  
- To:   *“We see risk building early, we act earlier, we protect citizens and budgets.”*

The **Risk Engine** is the heart of this vision.  
It turns millions of daily signals – behaviour, symptoms, devices, medications – into **safe, explainable, clinically useful risk insights** for:

- **Cardiovascular disease** (heart attack, stroke, hypertension)  
- **Metabolic disease** (type 2 diabetes and complications)  
- **Kidney disease (CKD)**  
- **Liver disease** (NAFLD/NASH trajectories)  
- **Respiratory disease** (COPD, asthma deterioration)

All of this is done in a way that:
- **never scares users**,  
- **empowers clinicians**, and  
- **gives ministries a clear, live picture of national health.**

### 2. What the Risk Engine Actually Does

The AHE Risk Engine continuously combines:

- **Long-term risk factors**  
  Age, sex, BMI, blood pressure, heart rate, smoking, activity level, sleep, alcohol pattern, family history.

- **Symptoms & Patient-Reported Outcomes (PROs)**  
  Chest pain, breathlessness, fatigue, dizziness, swelling, sleep problems, nightmares, bowel habit changes, appetite, thirst, anxiety.

- **Device & wearable signals**  
  Apple Health, Google Fit and medical devices (HR, HRV, steps, blood pressure, SPO2, temperature, sleep duration and quality).

- **Medication & treatment data**  
  Especially GLP-1 and other chronic disease therapies, adherence patterns, side-effects, benefits and weight trajectories from Phase 1–4.

These signals are:

1. **Normalised and mapped** into a clean risk input model.  
2. Passed through **clinically explainable rule-based models** (v1.0).  
3. Scored into **traffic-light risk levels** (green / amber / red).  
4. **Softened and safeguarded** by the Global Safety & Crisis Shield (Block G).  
5. Sent to:
   - Users → calm, non-frightening guidance.  
   - Clinicians → full analytical details.  
   - Ministries / insurers → anonymised cohort dashboards.

### 3. Why Elisence is Different

Most “symptom checkers” failed because they:

- frightened users with “cancer / heart attack” messages,  
- asked long, exhausting questionnaires,  
- gave low-value outputs to ministries.

**Elisence does the opposite:**

1. **Human experience first**  
   - For citizens: ultra-simple, conversational, yes/no/tap/speech.  
   - For clinicians: one clean dashboard per patient, with risk trends and explanations.  
   - For ministries: live cohort-level charts, maps and heat-maps.

2. **Explainable by design**  
   Every risk output can answer:  
   > “Why did the system classify this patient as amber or red?”  
   with a clear list of contributing factors and trends.

3. **Safety above all**  
   - No direct “diagnosis”.  
   - No catastrophic phrases sent to patients.  
   - Every message passes through a Safety & Crisis Shield that softens language and recommends safe actions.

4. **Ready for Population Health**  
   - Designed from day one for **national dashboards**:  
     prevalence, risk clusters, regional hotspots, program impact, cost-avoidance potential.

### 5. What Ministries, Hospitals and Insurers Receive

#### 5.1 For Ministries & National Programs

- **National Risk Dashboard**  
  - Live view of cardio / metabolic / CKD / liver / respiratory risk across regions.  
  - Heatmaps by age, gender, region, BMI band, disease cluster.  
  - Early-warning indicators (rising risk in specific sub-populations).

- **Program Impact Analytics**  
  - “Before / after” risk trends for supported medications (e.g. GLP-1), lifestyle programs, screening campaigns.  
  - Ability to answer:  
    > “Is this program truly lowering future risk – and for whom?”

- **Policy Decision Support**  
  - Evidence for where to invest next:  
    walkability, sleep campaigns, nutrition programs, targeted screening.

#### 5.2 For Hospitals & Clinicians

- **Doctor Passport**  
  - One clean view of each patient’s risk: trendline, drivers, medications, behaviour, devices.  
  - Comparisons with similar patients (age, gender, disease cluster).  
  - Clear traffic-light status and explanations.

- **Panel Management View**  
  - For each doctor: list of patients who are moving from green→amber or amber→red.  
  - Helps focus limited clinical time where it matters most.

#### 5.3 For Insurers & Payers

- **Population Risk Distribution**  
  - Segment insured members by risk cluster.  
  - Identify high-risk but modifiable groups.  

- **Prevention ROI Estimation**  
  - Simulate the effect of covering or expanding preventive programs on future risk.

### 6. Safety, Privacy & Regulatory Position

- **No black-box surprises**  
  v1.0 is rule-based and explainable.  
  ML models (v2.0) are optional and only used in clinician / ministry views, never directly for users.

- **Safety Shield for users**  
  - All messages go through a global Safety & Crisis Shield.  
  - No catastrophic or diagnostic wording is sent to citizens.  
  - The system encourages calm, supportive next steps (“speak with your doctor”, “book a check-up”, “improve sleep and activity”).

- **Privacy by architecture**  
  - Patient identifiers stay in-region.  
  - Aggregated, anonymised data can be used for regional / global benchmarking if the country agrees.  
  - Starts from GDPR / UK DPA level, then adapts to local regulation.

- **Audit & Governance**  
  - Each risk calculation is logged with provenance.  
  - Versioned models with clear change-history enable regulatory review and clinical governance.

### 7. Deployment & Partnership Model

Elisence AHE is designed to be:

- **Cloud or on-premise** – depending on national regulation.  
- **Modular** – ministries can start with one cluster (e.g. cardio+metabolic) and expand later.  
- **API-first** – can connect to existing EHR, national registries, citizen apps, and wearable platforms.

Typical partnership path:

1. **Pilot** – small-scale deployment with 1–3 hospitals or a high-risk population (e.g. obesity/diabetes program).  
2. **Evaluation** – compare risk trends, hospitalisations, and program engagement vs. historical baselines.  
3. **Scale-up** – national or regional rollout with ministry and insurers.  
4. **Continuous Improvement** – introduce ML v2.0, local research collaborations, and new disease clusters.
